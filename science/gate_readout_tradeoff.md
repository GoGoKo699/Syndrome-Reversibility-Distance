# Protected-gate locality and syndrome readout

The same stabilizer code supports both a protected logical Hamiltonian and a prescribed syndrome measurement. Combining the interaction-weight moments with the syndrome logical-circuit characterization gives a constraint on these two tasks: at odd distance, attaining the minimum interaction order forces the smallest finite syndrome reversibility distance allowed by parity.

This account connects [Error-Transparent Locality](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Error-Transparent-Locality) and [Syndrome Reversibility Distance](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Syndrome-Reversibility-Distance). It is a corollary of their existing theorems with an explicit proof of the fixed-qubit reduction. The two operational models retain their separate assumptions.

## 1. Notation and hypotheses

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $S$, $N$ | Phase-free stabilizer group and Pauli centralizer |
| $d$ | Ordinary detection distance of a stabilizer code encoding at least one qubit |
| $t$ | Largest protected tensor-Pauli error weight, with $1\le t$ and $2t+1\le d$ |
| $\ell$ | Maximum weight of a nonzero Pauli term in the physical Hamiltonian |
| $r_S$ | Syndrome reversibility distance, denoted $r(S)$ in the readout project |

The symbol $\ell$ replaces the gate project's $r$ to avoid confusing interaction order with readout distance. The code is fixed. The Hermitian Hamiltonian $H$ preserves the code and has a nonscalar logical generator $h_L$. For every Pauli $E$ of weight at most $t$, exact transparency requires

```math
HEV=E V(h_L+\lambda_E I),
```

where $V$ is an encoding isometry, $h_L=V^\dagger HV$, $\lambda_I=0$, and consistent real scalar sector shifts $\lambda_E$ are allowed. See the [gate model](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Error-Transparent-Locality/blob/main/science/model.md).

Independently, the readout experiment applies a specified product unitary, measures the full original stabilizer syndrome ideally, and then permits an arbitrary recovery that knows the unitary and outcome. Its invariant is

```math
r_S=\min_{\substack{g\in S,\ L\in N\setminus S\\
\operatorname{supp}(g)=\operatorname{supp}(L)}}\operatorname{wt}(gL),
\qquad \min\varnothing=\infty.
```

The [readout model](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Syndrome-Reversibility-Distance/blob/main/science/model.md) fixes that measurement order and recovery knowledge. A logical circuit is a nontrivial logical Pauli with no proper nonempty normalizer subword, retaining its fixed local Pauli labels. The [circuit theorem](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Syndrome-Reversibility-Distance/blob/main/science/support_criterion.md#4-equivalent-characterization-by-even-logical-circuits) identifies $r_S$ with the minimum even weight of such a circuit. In particular, finite $r_S$ is even and at least $d$.

## 2. Joint theorem

**Theorem.** Under the hypotheses above, let $d\ge3$ be odd. If a nontrivial error-transparent Hamiltonian attains the interaction-order lower bound, then

```math
\boxed{\ell=d+t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_S=d+1.}
```

Consequently, on a code with $r_S>d+1$, including $r_S=\infty$, every such Hamiltonian must satisfy

```math
\boxed{\ell\ge d+t+1.}
```

This is a necessary condition on any claimed equality construction, not an existence theorem for higher-error saturation. For $d=2t+1$, saturation at $\ell=3t+1$ forces $r_S=2t+2$. For even $d$, the readout theorem already gives $r_S=d$ without any Hamiltonian assumption.

### Step 1: remove fixed physical qubits

A weight-one stabilizer fixes its site in a pure Pauli eigenstate. Local Clifford changes of basis and qubit permutations preserve Pauli weight, the protected error set, distance, and $r_S$. After such changes, all fixed sites can be factored as

```math
V=V_{\mathrm{red}}\otimes|a\rangle,
\qquad
S=S_{\mathrm{red}}\times S_{\mathrm{fixed}},
```

where $S_{\mathrm{fixed}}$ is generated by single-site $Z$ labels and $|a\rangle$ is their prescribed product eigenstate. Clear their factors from the remaining stabilizer generators. The reduced code has no weight-one stabilizer and has the same $d$ and number of logical qubits: a nontrivial logical Pauli has a nontrivial restriction to the reduced code, and fixed-site factors can be removed without changing its logical class.

It also has the same $r_S$. A normalizer has only $I$ or $Z$ on each fixed site. In an equal-support stabilizer/logical pair, those factors agree exactly, so they cancel from the product and contribute no weight to the collision. Restriction gives a reduced collision of the same product weight. Conversely, every reduced collision extends by identity to the full code. This proves equality of the minima, including the empty case.

Take the **partial matrix element**, rather than a partial trace,

```math
H_{\mathrm{red}}=\langle a|H|a\rangle.
```

Each Pauli term either vanishes or becomes a term of no greater weight. Taking $\langle a|$ in the transparency identity for any error on the retained sites proves full transparency of $H_{\mathrm{red}}$ with the same $h_L$ and scalar shifts. Thus the reduction preserves the relevant hypotheses and has interaction order at most $d+t$.

### Step 2: minimum interaction order forces a weight-$(d+1)$ logical

Write the reduced Hamiltonian as $\sum_w H_w$, and remove scalar parts after compression:

```math
A_w=[V_{\mathrm{red}}^\dagger H_wV_{\mathrm{red}}]_0,
\qquad A=[h_L]_0\ne0.
```

The [weight-moment identities](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Error-Transparent-Locality/blob/main/science/locality.md) give

```math
A_w=0\ (w<d),\qquad
\sum_w A_w=A,\qquad
\sum_w w^jA_w=0\quad(1\le j\le t).
```

At most the $t+1$ weights $d,d+1,\ldots,d+t$ can contribute. None of these components can vanish: if only a set $\mathcal W$ of at most $t$ weights contributed, the polynomial

```math
q(x)=\prod_{w\in\mathcal W}(1-x/w)
```

would have degree at most $t$ and constant term one. The moments would imply $\sum_wq(w)A_w=A$, while all terms on the left vanish. This contradicts $A\ne0$.

In particular, $A_{d+1}\ne0$. A physical Pauli outside $N$ compresses to zero, and a stabilizer compresses to a scalar. Therefore some term of $H_{d+1}$ is a nontrivial logical Pauli $E$ of weight $d+1$ on the reduced code. For $t=1$, the familiar identities are $A_d=(d+1)A$ and $A_{d+1}=-dA$.

### Step 3: that logical operator is an even circuit

Suppose $E$ has a proper nonempty normalizer subword $F$. If $F$ is a stabilizer, then $\operatorname{wt}(F)\ge2$, because fixed qubits were removed. The complementary subword $EF$ would be a nontrivial logical Pauli of weight at most $d-1$, contradicting distance.

If $F$ is a nontrivial logical, its weight is at least $d$. Since it is proper in a word of weight $d+1$, its complementary subword has weight one and lies in $N$. Distance excludes a weight-one logical, while the reduction excludes a weight-one stabilizer. This is again impossible.

Hence $E$ is a logical circuit. Its weight $d+1$ is even. The circuit theorem gives $r_S\le d+1$, and parity together with distance gives the reverse inequality. Restoring the fixed qubits leaves $r_S$ unchanged, proving the theorem. The conditional stronger locality bound follows by combining this obstruction to equality with $\ell\ge d+t$. $\square$

### Which logical axes can be exposed?

At minimum interaction order, the [operator interpolation identity](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Error-Transparent-Locality/blob/main/science/coupling_cost.md#3-closed-cost-at-the-minimum-interaction-order) strengthens Step 2 to

```math
A_{d+1}=\alpha_1 A,\qquad
\alpha_1=-\frac{d(d+1)\cdots(d+t)}{(d+1)(t-1)!}\ne0.
```

Fix logical Pauli generators from $N/S$ and their resulting operator basis. Every nonzero Pauli component of $A$ therefore has a weight-$(d+1)$ representative in the reduced Hamiltonian. Each such representative is an even circuit by Step 3 and extends by identity to the original code. Thus every logical Pauli component present in the protected generator admits its own readout-loss witness. Different components can require different product rotations; this does not assert that one rotation measures all components or that every logical axis admits a protected gate.

## 3. The distance-three comparison

The [nine-qubit construction](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Error-Transparent-Locality/blob/main/science/four_body_construction.md) gives an explicit equality example. With its one-based site numbering, take

```math
E=Z_{1368},\qquad
g=X_{1246}X_{23458}=X_{13568},\qquad
L=gE=Y_1Y_3X_5Y_6Y_8.
```

Here $g$ is a stabilizer, $E$ is a representative of logical $Z$, and $L$ is a nontrivial logical Pauli. The displayed product has positive phase because four local $XZ=-iY$ factors occur. The supports of $g$ and $L$ are both $\{1,3,5,6,8\}$, while $\operatorname{wt}(gL)=4$. Thus this is a direct support-collision witness for $r_S=4$. Checking all fourteen proper nonempty subwords of $E$ also verifies the circuit condition.

| Code and protected gate | $\ell$ | $J/\omega$ | $r_S$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nine-qubit code, exhibited logical $Z$ rotation | 4 | 7 | 4 |
| Five-qubit code, canonical protected logical axis | 5 | 4 | $\infty$ |

The five-qubit code has only even-weight stabilizers and odd-weight nontrivial logical representatives, excluding equal supports. Its [canonical Hamiltonian](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Error-Transparent-Locality/blob/main/science/coupling_cost.md#the-five-qubit-benchmark) attains $\ell=5$ and $J/\omega=4$. Hence the stronger bound is sharp for $d=3,t=1,r_S=\infty$. The table compares these concrete constructions; it does not classify the full resource frontier or establish minimal block size or universal four-body control.

### A recoverability witness on the nine-qubit code

Rotate only sites $1,3,6,8$ about their $Z$ axes:

```math
U_\theta=\prod_{j\in\{1,3,6,8\}}\exp(-i\theta Z_j/2),
\qquad q=\sin^2(\theta/2).
```

The [even-circuit recovery formula](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Syndrome-Reversibility-Distance/blob/main/science/error_exponent.md) gives, for $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$,

```math
e_{\mathrm{opt}}(U_\theta)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}(U_\theta)
=3q^2-2q^3
=\frac{3}{16}\theta^4+O(\theta^6).
```

Both the optimal entanglement infidelity and optimal half-diamond error are attained by a logical $Z$ dephasing channel with this probability. This transfers the existing even-circuit formula to the protected-gate construction; it does not assert a new derivation of that recovery formula. For $0<\theta\le\pi/2$, the optimal error is positive despite allowing recovery to know the complete rotation. It is a post-measurement information loss, not an unknown-time single-fault gate error.

### Why arbitrary even logical weight is insufficient

Append a fixed $|0\rangle$ qubit to the five-qubit code. Multiplying a weight-three logical by the extra $Z$ stabilizer creates a weight-four logical, yet $r_S$ remains infinite. That word has a proper weight-one stabilizer subword and is not a circuit. This example explains why Step 1 cannot be omitted from the proof. It does not supply a four-body error-transparent gate.

## 4. Verification and remaining questions

[verify_gate_readout_tradeoff.py](../verification/verify_gate_readout_tradeoff.py) independently enumerates the complete stabilizer and centralizer of the nine-qubit, five-qubit, and fixed-ancilla codes. It computes $d$ and $r_S$ from their definitions and checks the explicit witness, its logical class, and the padding counterexample. The [verification guide](../verification/README.md) links the committed exact output and the existing full-Hamiltonian transparency checks. Identical copies of the standalone comparison script are included in both projects. Finite enumeration supports these examples; the all-code theorem is the proof above.

The immediate open directions are precise: determine when the stronger lower bound $\ell\ge d+t+1$ is attainable with $r_S>d+1$; determine which logical axes admit equality constructions; and analyze an explicitly specified experiment that combines gate faults with subsequent coherent rotations and readout. The present theorem does not combine those noise processes, constrain arbitrary circuits or changing encodings, or establish a decoder-efficiency or hardware advantage. Its readout asymptotics apply to each fixed code, with constants allowed to depend on the code.
